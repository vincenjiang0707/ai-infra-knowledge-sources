# Muni heritage weekend

I went to San Francisco for Muni Heritage Weekend 2026!
I stood by the tracks for three hours with my [line scan camera](https://daniel.lawrence.lu/blog/2026-06-12-standalone-cnc-line-scan-camera/), scanning trams.

Tram pics are freely licensed under CC-By-SA 4.0 (I’ve also uploaded a bunch to Wikimedia Commons). Eight of these photos are also available as large metal prints.

[1](https://daniel.lawrence.lu#s1) No. 1: Muni Streetcar 1 (1912)

[Muni No. 1](https://www.streetcar.org/streetcars/1-1-muni-1912/) is the first publicly owned big city streetcar in the US.
Before then, all streetcars were privately owned.
It’s pretty cool!

[2](https://daniel.lawrence.lu#s2) No. 162: Muni (1914-1950s)

[Muni No. 162](https://www.streetcar.org/streetcars/162-162-muni-1950s-livery/) started out from 1914 and served until 1958.
It’s making its first Muni Heritage Weekend appearance since a 2014 collision.
Sorry, there was a car in the background and someone bumped my camera, causing some vibrations.

[3](https://daniel.lawrence.lu#s3) No. 233: Blackpool Boat Tram (1934)

The [Blackpool boat tram](https://www.streetcar.org/streetcars/228-228-233-blackpool-england/) is known for looking like a boat with its open top design.

[4](https://daniel.lawrence.lu#s4) No. 496: Melbourne Tram (1928)

The [Melbourne tram](https://www.streetcar.org/streetcars/496-496-melbourne-australia/) comes from Melbourne, Australia.
Like No. 162, it is green.

[5](https://daniel.lawrence.lu#s5) No. 578: Dinky (1896)

[Muni No. 578](https://www.streetcar.org/streetcars/578-578s-msry-dinky/) is probably the oldest one.
It looks like a cable car but it is electrically powered rather than being powered by a cable.

Note that up until 1909, Divisadero Street used to be called [Devisadero Street](https://hoodline.com/2014/04/how-devisadero-became-divisadero/), so it’s a period-correct spelling, not a typo.

[6](https://daniel.lawrence.lu#s6) No. 1815: Milan Peter Witt (1928)

The [Milan tram](https://www.streetcar.org/streetcars/1895-1895-milan-orange/) was designed by Peter Witts so that it has more doors for rapid boarding.

I didn’t get a good line scan photo of this despite three attempts, which failed due to: out of focus, occluded by car, slightly out of focus and has a weird shadow on it. However, I did get a great pic with Pier 1 in the background.
