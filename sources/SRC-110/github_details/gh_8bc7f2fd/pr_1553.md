## 正文

## Summary

Adds **PLawBench**, a rubric-based benchmark for evaluating LLMs on real-world Chinese legal practice ([upstream repo](https://github.com/skylenage/PLawbench)). Every item carries a rubric annotated by legal experts, and grading is performed by an LLM judge against that rubric rather than against a reference answer.

Data is republished as [`evalscope/PLawBench`](https://modelscope.cn/datasets/evalscope/PLawBench) with **one ModelScope config per task**, so each task is a real subset rather than a client-side split.

## Subsets

The four subsets map one-to-one onto the leaf columns of the upstream leaderboard:

| Subset | N | Official column |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `legal_consultation` | 18 | Task1 |
| `case_analysis` | 250 | Task2-Avg, plus `conclusion_acc` / `fact_acc` / `reasoning_acc` / `law_acc` → Task2-Conclusion / Facts / Reasoning / Statute |
| `plaintiff_statement` | 6 | Task3-Plaintiff |
| `defendant_statement` | 6 | Task3-Defendant |

The three tasks use **different judge protocols** (different system prompts, different verdict schemas; only the consultation judge takes a question budget), so they are not a cosmetic split. Plaintiff and defendant are kept separate because the upstream leaderboard reports them as separate columns.

## Deviations from the official script

All are documented in the benchmark description:

- **Rubric denominators come from the dataset**, not the judge output, and awarded points are clamped to `[0, max_points]`. A judge that mis-reports the denominator therefore cannot distort the score.
- **The `case_analysis` judge template is repaired.** The official one ships malformed JSON (missing comma before `"others"`) and pins the conclusion section to `"total_points": 0`.
- **Judge verdicts are parsed with a string-aware brace matcher plus a repair pass** that closes unterminated strings/arrays/objects. This is needed in practice: real judge output was observed leaving the `suggestions` array unclosed, which would otherwise discard a sample whose `score_details` was complete and valid. The official script relies on `json_repair`, which is not a declared EvalScope dependency, so the repair is self-contained.

Task prompts and judge prompts are otherwise ported from the official release and verified byte-identical (modulo trailing whitespace stripped by the pre-commit hook).

## Reporting caveat

`OVERALL` is a per-sample mean, so `case_analysis` dominates it (250 of 280 samples). The paper's `Overall` column is an **equal-weighted mean of the three task scores** — fitted across the 24 models in the official ranking, equal-task weighting gives mean absolute error 0.72 versus 2.87 for a sample-weighted mean. The description tells users to compare per-subset scores instead. Per-subset scores are directly comparable to the paper.

## Verification

Real-API run across **all four subsets** (`qwen-plus` evaluated, `qwen3-max` as judge, `limit=6` — 100% of both drafting subsets):

| Subset | Num | mean_acc |
| --- | --- | --- |
| case_analysis | 6 | 0.8199 |
| legal_consultation | 6 | 0.8961 |
| plaintiff_statement | 6/6 | 0.5208 |
| defendant_statement | 6/6 | 0.8157 |
| **OVERALL** | 24 | **0.7631** |

- **Scores independently re-derived**: all 24 samples re-computed from the stored raw judge responses by a separate reimplementation using a *different* JSON parser (`json_repair`) — `mismatches=0`, `judge_failed=0`. Subset means and `OVERALL` also hand-checked.
- **Sanity signal**: the per-dimension ordering reproduces the published leaderboard, where `Facts` is the highest and `Statute` the lowest column for every model (here fact 0.989 > reasoning 0.789 > law 0.764 ≈ conclusion 0.700).
- **Dashboard verified end to end**: all 4 subsets render, the heterogeneous metric layout (5 metrics on `case_analysis`, 1 on the others) renders without blank cells or broken tables, radar chart draws, Chinese legal text and the rubric breakdown render readably, no JS console errors.
- `make lint` clean. Tests are end-to-end only, in `tests/benchmark/test_eval.py`: `test_plawbench_mock` (mock model + mock judge, no network) and `test_plawbench` (real API). `tests/cli/test_all.py::TestRun::test_ci_lite` also passes.

## Notes for reviewers

- Requires an LLM judge (`judge_strategy='llm'` or `'auto'`); `'rule'` raises a clear error.
- The drafting subsets ask for a 2,500–3,000 character document, so the **evaluated model** needs a generous `max_tokens` — a truncated filing is graded as incomplete and scores near zero for reasons unrelated to legal ability. This is called out in the description; it was observed in the verification run.
- `case_analysis` and `legal_consultation` are sampled, not exhaustively evaluated (a full 280-sample run is roughly 2h of judge calls).
- LLM judges are not bit-deterministic even at `temperature=0`; the drafting subsets moved by ~0.04 between two scoring passes over identical predictions.
