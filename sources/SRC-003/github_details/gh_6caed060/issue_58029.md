# [Issue #58029] [Bug]: Whisper `verbose_json` silently drops the words after the last complete segment at every chunk cut (long audio); `json`/`text` keep them

source: https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/58029
state: open | updated: 2026-09-23T02:23:57Z
labels: 

## 正文


### Your current environment

vLLM 0.25.1 (`vllm/vllm-openai:v0.25.1`), `openai/whisper-large-v3`, RTX 5070 Ti, `--dtype float32` (also reproduces in float16), `/v1/audio/transcriptions`.

### 🐛 Describe the bug

For audio longer than `max_audio_clip_s` (30 s) vLLM splits it into contiguous chunks at the quietest point of the last second of each window (`split_audio`). When that cut lands mid-sentence, Whisper decoded **with timestamp tokens** (which `verbose_json` requires) closes its last complete segment and emits nothing for the audio after it. The words between that last segment end and the cut are lost from `segments` **and** from the `text` field of the verbose response, which is assembled from segments. `response_format=json` / `text` decode with `<|notimestamps|>` and keep those words.

This is not the timestamp drift of #32588 and not the split-point scan bug of #52927 (PR #52927 changes *where* the cut lands; the loss happens whenever a cut lands inside speech at all).

**Minimal reproduction** (synthetic speech, no data-privacy concerns; the split is deterministic):

```bash
espeak-ng -v en-us -s 160 -w long.wav "Chief complaint. The patient is a sixty two year old woman who presents for follow up of type two diabetes and hypertension. She reports good adherence to metformin one thousand milligrams twice daily and losartan fifty milligrams daily. History of present illness. Over the past three months she has noted increased fatigue and occasional blurry vision in the mornings. She denies polyuria, polydipsia, chest pain, or shortness of breath. Home glucose readings range from one hundred thirty to one hundred ninety milligrams per deciliter. Review of systems. Positive for fatigue and intermittent headaches."
ffmpeg -y -i long.wav -ac 1 -ar 16000 -sample_fmt s16 -t 30 first30.wav      # exactly one 30 s window
for fmt in json verbose_json; do
  curl -s -F "file=@first30.wav;type=audio/wav" -F model=openai/whisper-large-v3 -F response_format=$fmt \
       http://localhost:8000/v1/audio/transcriptions | python3 -c "import json,sys; t=json.load(sys.stdin)['text']; print('$fmt', len(t.split()), 'words:', t[-120:])"
done
```

Observed:

```
json          67 words: ... chest pain, or shortness of breath. Home glucose readings range  Fru-
verbose_json  59 words: ... occasional blurry vision in the mornings. She denies polyuria, polydipsia, chest pain,  FRU-
```

`verbose_json`'s last real segment ends at 26.4 s; the 3.6 s of speech before the 30 s cut ("or shortness of breath. Home glucose readings range") is gone. On a 977 s pause-free clip this happened at 4 of 32 chunk boundaries (11.3 s of audio, ~1.4 % of the words); on a real 16-minute dictation with natural pauses, 2 of the cuts still landed inside speech.

### Why

The reference implementations (openai/whisper, faster-whisper) decode a 30 s window, take the timestamp of the last complete segment, and **seek the next window to that timestamp**, re-decoding the tail with fresh context. vLLM pre-cuts the audio into fixed contiguous chunks before decoding, so the tail after the last segment is never decoded by anyone. Feeding previous-chunk context (#25750, PR #20249) would improve accuracy but does not recover those words either.

### Proposed fixes (either would do)

```
1. **Seek-based chunking for timestamp requests**: decode chunk *i*; if its output ends with a closed segment at *t* < chunk end, start chunk *i+1* at *t* (not at the energy-based cut). This is what the reference decoders do and makes the loss structurally impossible. It serializes chunks, which is fine for verbose requests (they already are sequential in the offline path).
2. **Cheaper mitigation**: when the model's output for a chunk ends without a closing timestamp *or* ends more than N s before the chunk end, re-decode the uncovered tail `[t, chunk_end)` as an extra request and append it as a segment.
```

Workaround we use today: cut the audio at silences into ≤ 28 s pieces client-side and send each piece separately (the server never chunks), then offset the segment times. Works, but every verbose_json user of long audio is hitting this without knowing.

(The +1…+6 on the short clips are words the `json` decode itself dropped at its end, e.g. first30's `json` stops at "...readings range" this time; verbose now has "...range from". So those are the verbose output being *more* complete, not duplication. Seam pre-roll shows up as segments overlapping by 0.5 s, which consumers should tolerate.)

**Seek-based fixes the boundary loss but opens a different hole.** On the 16-minute clip the window starting at 689.22 s produced one segment (689.22–692.82 "Monofilament testing intact. Assessment and plan.") and then ended on a *single* timestamp, so `_get_verbose_seek_offset` returned `None` and the next window started at 719.22 s. 26.4 s of speech (≈58 words) vanished, which is more than stock loses on that clip. That is exactly the "decoder walks away from the rest of the chunk" case #53144 describes, and the reference decoders have the same blind spot (openai-whisper also advances a full window on a single-timestamp ending). So seek-based chunking alone cannot be the whole fix: it still needs a tail re-decode for windows the model abandons. It also serialises the windows, which cost 2.6× on the 16-minute clip (16.2 s vs 6.1 s); the parallel chunk path is one of vLLM's real advantages for long audio.

**Tail-repair (#53145) recovered every stretch on these clips** (log: "3 abandoned stretch(es), 3 recovered" on the 16-minute clip) and keeps the parallel decode, so wall time is within 8 % of stock. The three remaining lost words sit at the very end: the last chunk's abandoned tail is 2.32 s by the model's timestamp but the chunk itself is slightly shorter than 30 s (energy-based cut), so it lands just under `UNDECODED_MIN_GAP_S = 2.0` on 0.25.1's `idx * 30 s` offsets. On main with exact `chunk_start_offsets` that arithmetic is tighter; a threshold nearer 1.5 s would also catch it.

My read: the seek-based idea is right for *where the next window starts* (it is what removes the mid-sentence cut in the first place), but it needs #53145's abandoned-tail recovery, or #53145 alone is the more complete fix as it stands. Happy to re-run either on sm_120 once there is a PR; the bench and the ported patches are in my repro kit if useful.

### ravo42-maker · 2026-09-23

@HritickGokul my recommendation is to put the effort into #53145 rather than a hybrid, at least for this bug.

Reasoning:

```
- #53145 already fixes what this issue reports (every chunk-cut loss on the repro clips came back) *and* #53144, without touching the chunking strategy or giving up the parallel chunk decode. It still merges cleanly against main as of today (`mergeable: true`, state `blocked`), so what it is missing is not a rebase but a maintainer review, a `/ci run`, and a regression test. It has been open since Aug 20 with no review yet; #53236, the other attempt at the same repair, was closed.
- A hybrid (seek the next window to the last closed timestamp *and* re-decode abandoned tails) would give the cleanest text, but it changes the chunker, which is the same design conversation as #52927 / #32588 and belongs under the tracking issue #25750 as a follow-up. Landing it as a second step on top of #53145 is also easier to review than one PR that does both. The 2.6x serial cost on long audio would need an answer first (e.g. seek-based only for `verbose_json`, or keeping N chunks in flight and re-seeking only the seam), and that is a bigger change than this bug needs.
```

Concrete things that would move #53145 forward, if Perlover is open to it:

```
1. **Your regression test.** The 30 s / 26.4 s single-timestamp case is exactly the abandoned-stretch path, so the same test should pass on #53145 unchanged. A test that does not need a real model (a fake generator that emits a lone timestamp before EOT) is what the reviewers will want, and it is what the PR currently lacks.
2. **The last-chunk threshold.** The 3 words still lost on the 16-minute clip are the final chunk's abandoned 2.32 s landing just under `UNDECODED_MIN_GAP_S = 2.0` once the energy-based cut makes the chunk shorter than 30 s. Worth checking on main whether the exact `chunk_start_offsets` already catch it; if not, 1.5 s, or no minimum on the final chunk (nothing follows it, so there is no "breath between segments" to protect), would close it.
3. **Segment monotonicity.** Recovered segments start `UNDECODED_PREROLL_S` before the previous segment's end (26.42 -> 25.92 on my run). Clamping the recovered segment's `start` to the previous `end` after `strip_seam_overlap` runs would keep `segments` monotonic for consumers that assume it, at no cost to the text.
```

I will re-run the bench on sm_120 against whatever revision comes out of that; the clips and scripts are deterministic, so the numbers above are directly comparable. If it helps the reviewers, I can also post a before/after on a real 16-minute dictation (word counts and boundary-gap positions only, no transcript).
