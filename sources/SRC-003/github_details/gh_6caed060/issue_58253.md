# [Issue #58253] [RFC]: Increase vLLM Review Capacity with a Reviewer Role

state: open | updated: 2026-09-24T15:18:04Z

## 正文

## Motivation

vLLM’s contribution volume exceeds its available review and triage capacity, creating a massive PR backlog. To help address this backlog and stem its future growth, we propose a **Reviewer** role to expand the community of trusted reviewers who take ownership of triage and initial review, helping authors bring their PRs up to vLLM’s standards. Today, much of that responsibility falls on committers, who must balance review with implementation, architectural decisions, releases, and maintenance.

The growing volume of low-quality AI-generated contributions (“AI slop”) compounds this problem, increasing the effort required to identify worthwhile changes and bring them up to project standards. The Reviewer title also recognizes review as a valuable contribution and establishes a clearer path toward committership.

## The Reviewer Role

Reviewers take ownership of triage and initial review, helping contributors bring their PRs up to vLLM’s standards. This includes understanding the problem, evaluating the proposed solution, examining correctness and validation, and working with authors to resolve concerns. Reviewers help address common issues that consume review capacity, including unnecessary complexity, poorly scoped changes, overspecified tests, unjustified modifications to core code, and missing performance measurements or accuracy evaluations. Addressing these issues is especially valuable as AI-generated contributions increase.

Reviewer status formally recognizes contributors’ review work and the trust they have earned within the project. Following [Kubernetes’ reviewer/approver model](https://github.com/kubernetes/community/blob/b8ce3416617273bfb8611b18b4cd8412893e44db/community-membership.md), authors are expected to address reviewer feedback by making changes or responding with an explanation before requesting final committer review. Outstanding concerns must be resolved, or explicitly adjudicated by a committer, before the PR is merged. Reviewers should involve relevant committers when questions require their input.

The formal title signals to contributors that reviewer feedback is part of the project’s acceptance process and must be addressed. It also signals to other committers that they can rely on reviewers’ judgment within the scope of the review they performed.

Reviewers can:

- Review PRs and provide a recognized approval or request changes.  
- Trigger CI when a contribution is ready for evaluation.  
- Escalate design questions to the appropriate committer and SIG.  
- Triage PRs/issues and apply appropriate labels; flagging potentially important PRs that should receive expedited committer attention.  
- Close duplicate PRs/issues to cut down noise and focus effort on the most promising contributions.  
- Flag PRs that are ready for committer review, approval, and merge.

Reviewers cannot:

- Merge PRs into main themselves, even when approved and CI is green.

A completed reviewer pass should give the committer a useful starting point: what was checked, what evidence is available, and what still needs committer attention. Ideally, committer review should be minimal at this point, mostly an LGTM and final approval, with substantive concerns already addressed during reviewer-led triage and initial review.

Substantive requests for changes must be resolved before merge, either through revisions or an explicit resolution by a committer. Authors may ask for clarification or escalate disagreements; reviewer status does not make every preference a requirement. Reviewer approval signals readiness for final consideration. It does not replace committer review or guarantee acceptance.

## Assigning Reviewers

A list of active reviewers will be made public in [https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/community/reviewers](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/community/reviewers) and will include recommendations on their areas of expertise/knowledge. Reviewers should self-assign PRs they are actively reviewing and publicly coordinate with other reviewers in \#pr-reviews. 

## Appointment and Development

Candidates should demonstrate a sustained record of useful reviews, sound engineering judgment, constructive communication, and familiarity with vLLM’s contribution standards. Deep ownership of a subsystem is not required. A reviewer should be nominated by 2 committers from different organizations. Committers should post in \#committers when nominating reviewers.

Before being nominated a general rule of thumb (not a hard requirement) are the reviewer should have either 15 merged PRs and have reviewed\* 30 PRs OR have reviewed\* 45 PRs.

\* “Reviewed” implies the reviewer gave a substantive review and the PR went on to some kind of resolution (merged or closed)

New reviewers should receive feedback and mentorship from committers. A private Slack channel will be set up to facilitate communication between Reviewers and Committers, including review questions, feedback, and coordination on PRs.

First serving as a reviewer will become the standard path for new committer appointments. This establishes demonstrated review service as part of the path to merge authority. While this is not a hard requirement and there may be exceptions we do consider this the standard and preferred path.

## Rollout and Evaluation

Start with a small cohort of trusted reviewers working across the project (initially \~50, potentially scaling up to 2x active committers).

Things to assess:

- Time to first substantive review and time waiting for final committer review.  
- Review quality, i.e. committer effort required to bring reviewed contributions to a final decision.  
- CI queue pressure.  
- Backlog size and age, distinguishing merged PRs from closed or withdrawn PRs.

Success is a sustained increase in effective review capacity: contributions receive useful feedback sooner, review responsibility is distributed across more trusted contributors, and the backlog declines without compromising quality or overloading reviewers and CI.

Community members should have access to a directory of which reviewers to reach out to for different topics, to enable them collaborate effectively on slack channels such as \#pr-reviews

## Precedents

Several established projects distribute review responsibilities beyond contributors with final acceptance or merge authority:

```
- [**Kubernetes**](https://github.com/kubernetes/community/blob/main/community-membership.md#reviewer) distinguishes Reviewers, who assess contribution quality and correctness, from Approvers, who authorize acceptance.  
- [**containerd**](https://github.com/containerd/.project/blob/main/GOVERNANCE.md#reviewers) has explicit Reviewer and Committer tiers. Reviewers’ LGTMs count toward merge requirements, but reviewers do not have write access. Reviewer service is part of the path toward committership.  
- [**Python / CPython**](https://devguide.python.org/triage/triage-team/) has a formal Triage Team whose responsibilities include reviewing PRs, assisting contributors, and involving core developers. Its stated purpose is to provide timely feedback and reduce core-developer workload.
```

## Implementation

The implementation must give reviewer decisions meaningful visibility while keeping final acceptance and merging with committers.

### Option A \[Selected\]: Native GitHub reviews with restricted merging

Give Reviewers Write access, and restrict updates to protected branches to the Committer team and authorized bots. This gives reviewer feedback native GitHub review authority, including blocking requests for changes under the configured review rules.

This option uses GitHub’s existing review interface without requiring a bot to recognize reviewer approvals. It does grant broader write privileges, including on unprotected branches. All relevant branches and merge paths must therefore be covered by the restrictions, and the configuration must be verified before rollout. GitHub documents these capabilities in its [repository role permissions](https://docs.github.com/en/organizations/managing-user-access-to-your-organizations-repositories/managing-repository-roles/repository-roles-for-an-organization) and [branch protection rules](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/configuring-branches-and-merges-in-your-repository/managing-protected-branches/about-protected-branches).

```python
### Option B: Triage access with a bot-managed review check
```

Give Reviewers Triage access and use a bot to recognize reviews from the designated Reviewer team. The bot would publish a named status check, such as “Reviewer approval,” and track unresolved requests for changes.

The bot would make Reviewer approvals visible as a readiness signal without requiring them for merge. It would separately track unresolved Reviewer requests for changes so those concerns must be resolved or explicitly adjudicated by a Committer. A bot-generated check would not change the permissions or appearance of the native GitHub review. This option preserves the absence of repository write access but requires automation that handles team membership, updated commits, dismissed reviews, outstanding objections, and explicit committer resolutions correctly.

#### Review Automation Options

##### Prow

[Kubernetes’ Prow](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/prow) provides an established command model:

- `/cc @username`: request review from a contributor.  
- `/assign @username`: assign responsibility for a PR.  
- `/lgtm`: record that a change looks good following review.  
- `/lgtm cancel`: withdraw that review signal.  
- `/approve`: record final acceptance by an authorized approver.  
- `/hold`: prevent automatic merging while an outstanding concern is resolved.

For vLLM, `/lgtm` could represent a trusted Reviewer or Committer’s completed review, while `/approve` would remain restricted to Committers. Committers could perform both steps directly. Prow can also translate native GitHub approval and request-changes reviews into LGTM label changes.

Prow’s default authorization does not exactly match this proposal: `/lgtm` is generally available to repository collaborators, while `/approve` uses OWNERS files.

##### Mergeable

[Mergeable](https://mergeable.readthedocs.io/en/latest/validators/approval.html) can react to native GitHub reviews through `pull_request_review.*`, count approvals from designated teams, and publish a review check. Its [approval validator](https://github.com/mergeability/mergeable/blob/master/lib/validators/approvals.js) filters reviews by team membership or username without requiring write access, making it a candidate for recognizing Triage-only Reviewer approvals. Reviewers could use GitHub’s normal Approve and Request changes actions, with a passing check signaling readiness for committer review. 

##### A lightweight GitHub App or GitHub Actions workflow

Custom automation could recognize native reviews or a small command set from explicit Reviewer and Committer teams, publish review status, and integrate with existing CI labels and triggers. This offers the most direct fit for Option B but makes vLLM responsible for maintaining the implementation.

### CI access

The implementation should verify the behavior of labels and CI triggers and build on existing controls where possible. CI access should allow reviewers to unblock ready contributions without routinely involving a committer. Reviewers should assess readiness before triggering expensive jobs and avoid unnecessary reruns. Faster review may increase CI demand even if incoming PR volume stays constant. The rollout should monitor queue times and resource use.
