## 正文

**Update 2026-09-24**: real-server A/B + e2e fidelity experiments done (Qwen3.8-27B hybrid, 2×910B3). Conclusion up front: **the retreat scenario is real and large (93% of requests, 30% of prefill compute), but both candidate replay mechanisms are economically unviable on interleaved hybrids — the correct lever is checkpoint placement/retention policy in the existing engine, not a new execution path.** Details and tables below. Instrumentation: #41001.

**Area**: `python/sglang/srt/mem_cache/` (UnifiedRadixCache, MambaComponent, schedule_batch) · Related: #37424, #37795, #37091–#37914, #39115, #28511, #38652

## Problem

On hybrid models, when the Mamba checkpoint behind a full-KV hit is missing or evicted, the engine retreats the whole hit to that checkpoint and re-prefills the gap through all layers — even though the gap's full-KV is already cached.

## A/B results (150-request agentic traces per cell, first-admission semantics)

**1. Where the problem lives — retreat rate by pressure type**

| Pressure | Requests retreating (H > C) | Gap (H−C) p50 / max | Replay-eligible tokens | TTFT p50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none (control) | 0% | — | 0 | 2.5s |
| full-KV capacity (112K) | **0%** (whole nodes evicted, `H == C`) | — | 0 | 46.4s |
| Mamba pool = 48 | 0% | — | 0 | 2.5s |
| Mamba pool = 32 | 4% | 22K / 50K | 156K | 2.5s |
| Mamba pool = 24 | 79% | 32K / 50K | 3.53M | — |
| Mamba pool = 16 | **93%** | 32K / 49K | **4.12M** | 49.3s |
| 12 sessions, pool = 32 | 92% | 25K / 66K | 7.88M | — |
| 12 sessions, pool = 16 | 96% | 16K / 49K | 8.16M | — |

→ The problem requires **Mamba pool pressure**, not full-KV pressure: full-KV eviction removes whole nodes (`H == C` by construction) and hides the gap entirely.

**2. What the retreat costs — avoidable compute** (modeled from measured TTFT grids; "avoided" = if every winnable gap were repaired)

| Cell | Gap tokens to repair | Modeled re-prefill time | Avoided compute |
|---|---|---|---|
| mb32 | 156K | 314s → 301s | **4.3%** |
| mb24 | 3.53M | 875s → 608s | **30.5%** |
| mb16 (seed 0) | 4.12M | 1422s → 973s | **31.6%** |
| mb16 (seed 1) | 3.84M | 914s → 624s | **31.7%** |
| mb16 (seed 2) | 3.99M | 940s → 644s | **31.5%** |
| 12 sessions, pool 32 | 7.88M | 1885s → 1310s | **30.5%** |
| 12 sessions, pool 16 | 8.16M | 1891s → 1318s | **30.3%** |

**3. Output fidelity — e2e greedy continuation, 32 tokens** (noise floor = flush-cache fresh-vs-fresh)

| Tier | Setup | Exact match | Mean match |
|---|---|---|---|
| Noise floor | two fresh prefills, cache flushed | **28/28** | 32/32 |
| Leaf-checkpoint seed | clean full hit, GDN from donated state | **14/20** | diverge ≤ 23 tok |
| Aged-checkpoint replay | retreat to checkpoint C < H | env-sensitive* | — |

\* Instrumented state diff confirms the mechanism: divergence occurs iff the seed position ≠ the reference's live position; same-position seeds are 100% token-identical. Two upstream-facing findings: (a) track placement is nondeterministic across identical runs (same prompt: 2816 / 2688 / none), (b) GDN state numerics are chunk-decomposition sensitive. Both already affect today's clean-hit path.

## Why replay is economically unviable here (corrects the original proposal)

Qwen3.5 interleaves GGGF (48 GDN + 16 FA): every GDN layer consumes the previous layer's output.

| Candidate mechanism | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Split-prefix extend (FA at `H`, GDN replays `[C, P)`) | Once FA skips `[C, H)`, later GDN layers have no hidden states to replay with. Running FA over `[C, H)` anyway costs the same as today — **saving zero**. |
| ReplaySSM-style ring reconstruction (#28511) | Ring records ≈ 0.8 MB/token across 48 layers → covering a measured 32K gap costs **~25 GB per request**, vs ~13s of re-prefill compute. **Memory cost exceeds the compute it avoids by orders of magnitude.** |

The corollary: today's retreat (all-layer re-prefill of `[C, P)`) is already compute-optimal for this architecture — every FLOP in it is necessary. The waste is not in *how* the gap is repaired but in *that the gap exists at all*.

## Revised proposal

1. **Observability (land now)**: first-admission retreat metrics — `mamba_retreat_requests_total`, `mamba_retreat_gap_tokens_total`, `mamba_retreat_collapse_requests_total`, gap histogram, `mamba_replay_would_admit_total`. Working implementation + unit tests on fork branch `rfc40865-phase1-retreat-metrics` (instrumentation in #41001). This lets every deployment quantify its own retreat rate before any policy change.
2. **Checkpoint retention policy (the real lever)**: keep the gap small instead of repairing it — pin the most recent K checkpoints per active session (78 MB/slot at this model size, so pinning K=4 for 100 sessions ≈ 31 GB — a tunable trade vs 30% re-prefill), and/or use a denser track interval for active sessions so `C` tracks `H` closely. The engine already self-heals via force-track at the branching point (`_force_track_h`); under pool pressure those healing checkpoints get evicted, which is what turns one retreat into a persistent collapse — retention targets exactly that.
3. **Replay execution path: withdrawn.** Both mechanisms fail economically (table above). Keeping this RFC open as the measurement record for findings (a)/(b).

## Asks

1. OK to land the first-admission retreat metrics upstream (item 1)?
2. Interest in the checkpoint retention policy (item 2) — and where should it live: MambaComponent eviction ordering vs scheduler-side pinning?
3. Are findings (a) track-placement nondeterminism and (b) chunk-decomposition-dependent GDN numerics worth separate upstream issues? Happy to file with the measurement protocol and data.


## 评论 (1)

### curnane-lab · 2026-09-24

We ran the experiment behind this RFC on a real server with a real agentic trace. Headline: the retreat scenario is real, its trigger condition is more specific than the RFC guessed, and the modeled savings land at **~31% of total prefill compute time** under mamba-pool pressure with zero latency regressions.

**Two honesty labels up front**: (1) the savings below are *model-projected* — a microbenchmark-fitted cost model evaluated on measured (P, H, C) distributions — not end-to-end numbers from a split-prefix implementation; (2) "no regression" means *latency* non-regression. Output fidelity under replay is not covered by this experiment (the RFC's 93–99.9% figure is offline state-reconstruction fidelity); an on-server output-parity run is our next step.

## Setup

- Env: 8x Ascend 910B3, Qwen3.8-27B hybrid (48 GDN + 16 full-attn layers), TP2, sglang v0.5.18 + cherry-picks (@ 71de97b264). All cited mechanisms were cross-checked against main @ 224a247d5b — the retreat/branching semantics are identical; only file/line positions differ.
- Workload: CacheWise agentic multi-turn trace (shared prefix + small incremental appends) replayed against a real server, with a patch that logs P (prompt), H (full-KV hit), C (accepted mamba boundary) per match.
- Instrumentation branch: `curnane-lab/sglang@rfc40865-tailreplay-instrumentation-v2` (the trace patch; the counters are productizable as the RFC's metric set).
- Cost arms: Arm A = current behavior (retreat to C, re-prefill [C, P) through all layers). Arm B ≈ GDN-only replay cost, measured with FA layers skipped — a conservative approximation since GDN cost is context-independent and the FA segment is independent.

## Finding 1 — the trigger is mamba-pool pressure, not full-KV pressure

Full-KV pool pressure (`--max-total-tokens` at 112K units, 150 requests, 2724 matches) produced **zero** H>C events: tree nodes get evicted wholesale, so H==C by construction. The retreat scenario only appears under mamba-pool capacity pressure (`--max-mamba-cache-size`): the mamba pool (usage ≤0.14 throughout the full-KV run) evicts per-turn states under LRU while full-KV hits still cover the whole session history.

| unit | pressure | retreat rate | gap (H−C) p50/p90/max | replay-eligible tokens | share of re-prefill |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no-pressure | none | 0% | – | 0 | 0% |
| 112K | full-KV pool | 0% | – | 0 | 0% |
| mb32 | mamba pool 32 | 4% | 22.1K/38.4K/49.8K | 156K | 31% |
| mb16 | mamba pool 16 | **93%** | 31.9K/41.0K/49.2K | **4.12M** | **83%** |

(150 requests per unit; gaps far exceed "one grid" — with a small mamba pool, per-turn states are evicted and requests fall back to checkpoints tens of thousands of tokens behind while the full-KV hit still covers the session.)

## Finding 2 — the cost model's two premises hold

Microbenchmarks (GDN-only vs all-layer prefill over gap G at context C):

- **GDN-only replay is context-independent** (delta-rule constant state): G=49K is flat at ~8.6s for C ∈ {0, 16K, 32K, 65K}.
- **Current re-prefill grows quadratically in C** (FA over the prefix): G=49K goes 15.5s (C=0) → 54.3s (C=65K); the FA share replay eliminates is 6.9s → 45.7s per request.

## Finding 3 — projected savings, and they are stable

Policy `budget = max(min_tail, ratio·P)`, min_tail=1024, ratio=1.0:

| unit | retreat rate | replayed tokens | modeled prefill time | saved | per-retreated-request TTFT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mb32 | 4% | 156K | 314.1→300.6s | 4.3% | 2.26s (n=6) |
| mb16 | 93% | 4.12M | 1422.3→973.1s | **31.6%** | **3.23s mean (n=141)** |

Stability: three seeds at mb16 give 31.5–31.7% (±0.1pp); every unit with retreat rate >79% converges to 30.3–31.7%; 12-session units double the absolute eligible tokens (7.9–8.2M) at the same percentage. The retreat rate itself is threshold-like (mb48 0% → mb32 4% → mb24 79%): tail-replay is insurance that pays out ~30% of prefill time exactly when the mamba pool is undersized, and costs nothing when it isn't.

No replayed request regresses, and this is structural rather than lucky: both paths do identical GDN work over [C, P), and replay strictly removes the FA compute over [C, H). Model-vs-measured deltas are negative (queueing makes the real server slower than the model), so queue effects only amplify the relative win.

## Answers to the RFC asks

- **Ask 1 (budget shape)**: not `ratio·P` — in this regime gaps (32–50K) dwarf any proportional budget, and intermediate ratios (0.05–0.5) reject every winnable request. Since accept-is-strictly-a-win in this cost model, we recommend an **absolute cap** (`H−C ≤ SGLANG_MAMBA_REPLAY_TAIL_MAX`, env or server arg, protecting decode throughput and fidelity headroom) **gated on the mamba-pool watermark** — the signal that actually predicts retreat.
- **Ask 2 (charge placement)**: admission-time, keyed on the same watermark.
- **Metrics**: `mamba_replay_tokens_total`, gap (H−C) histogram, retreat/collapse counter — our trace patch is a working prototype of all three.

## Caveats and next steps

- Numbers are model projections, not end-to-end; the real split-prefix extend (FA from H, GDN seeded at C) remains the heavy item, as flagged in the RFC's execution-path note. We plan phase 1 = policy/charge/metrics (default-off), phase 2 = forward path.
