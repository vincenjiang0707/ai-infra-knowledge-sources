### Pull Request Description

> **Draft.** The API shape is being settled in #1418, where the proposal is posted. The commits are ordered API first, then the projection, then the wiring, with the zero-replica fix folded into the wiring commit. Integration and E2E tests follow as the last commits. This PR stays in draft and follows whatever is agreed in #1418.

#1418 asks for predictive autoscaling for LLM inference, where a pod start-up takes long
enough that reacting to a metric spike is always late. This change adds an opt-in
`spec.predictive` block to the `PodAutoscaler`. The controller projects the metric trend of
the observation window forward, records the projection in `status.predictive`, and, in `Auto`
mode, uses it as a floor for scale-up. The reactive path keeps its role: the projection can
raise a decision, never lower one, and the default mode changes nothing.

The claim comment on the issue mentioned sending this as separate PRs. It ships as one
because the three pieces are one API surface: piece 1 alone would merge `spec.predictive`
and `status.predictive` with nothing that writes the status, and the predictor has no caller
until the decision pipeline composes it. With `Preview` as the default mode, merging it
whole still ships no behaviour change until a workload opts into `Auto`, which is the
property the separate pieces were meant to protect.

On the two open questions from the claim comment: the projection lives in a `predictive`
sub-block rather than in `metricsSources`, because it composes with the sources instead of
replacing one; and the up/down philosophy is a safety floor rather than confidence
blending, so the reactive algorithm keeps deciding when capacity is released.

### Semantics

- **Preview first.** `mode` defaults to `Preview`, where the controller computes the
  projection and writes it to `status.predictive`, and the replica decision stays exactly as
  it is without the block. `Auto` turns the projection into a floor:
  `final = max(reactive, projected)`.
- **Up only.** The projection never triggers or lowers a scale-down, so reactive scaling
  keeps full control of releasing capacity. The floor is capped by the configured scale-up
  rate, and the result still passes through `minReplicas`, `maxReplicas`, the active
  `schedules` entry and the scale-up cooldown window.
- **An honest baseline.** The projection is a least-squares line over the samples inside
  `observeWindowSeconds`, evaluated at `horizonSeconds` (2 minutes when unset). It is
  clamped at zero and at four times the observed mean, and it is only produced from at least
  three samples spanning at least half of the window, so a fresh `PodAutoscaler` reports
  nothing until the history supports it.
- **Same formula as the reactive path.** A projected value becomes replicas with the formula
  of the active strategy, `ceil(value / targetValue)` for KPA and
  `ceil(currentReplicas * value / targetValue)` for APA, so the two numbers `Auto` compares
  are the same kind of number.
- **HPA is out of scope.** The HPA strategy delegates its decision to a native
  `HorizontalPodAutoscaler` resource, which has no channel to receive a projection, so the
  validating webhook rejects the combination instead of accepting a block that would do
  nothing.
- **No spec churn when it is off.** Without `spec.predictive` nothing is projected and
  `status.predictive` stays absent; removing the block clears it.

### Review guide

The diff is large because the API, the projection, the wiring, the generated artifacts and the tests land together. The three commits follow the same order as the list below. Reading order:

1. `api/autoscaling/v1alpha1/podautoscaler_types.go` (+63) - the whole API surface: `PredictiveSpec` (`mode`, `horizonSeconds`) and `PredictiveStatus` (`mode`, `observedValue`, `predictedValue`, `predictedReplicas`, `lastUpdated`). The status values are strings, matching how metric values are compared elsewhere in the API and keeping a float diff out of the status.
2. `pkg/controller/podautoscaler/prediction/predictor.go` (new, 201) with `predictor_test.go` - the projection: `Project` windows the samples and projects a least-squares line `horizon` forward, `leastSquares` is the fit, and the constants at the top of the file are the guards (minimum samples, minimum coverage, the clamp at zero, the growth cap).
3. `pkg/controller/podautoscaler/autoscaler.go` (+160) - where the projection joins the decision. `applyPredictiveFloor` is the merge (`max(reactive, projected)`), `evaluatePrediction` picks the projection that asks for the most replicas, and `predictedReplicas` converts a value with the formula of the active strategy. `ComputeDesiredReplicas` gains one call after the pending-replica guard; the reactive algorithms themselves are not modified.
4. `pkg/controller/podautoscaler/podautoscaler_controller.go` (+55/-3) - the only edited lines in the PR: the `setStatus` signature and its three call sites now carry the evaluation, and `predictiveStatus`, `keepPredictiveObservationTime` and `formatMetricValue` write it. `lastUpdated` only moves when the projection changes, so a steady series does not rewrite the status on every resync.
5. `pkg/webhook/podautoscaler_webhook.go` (+35) with `podautoscaler_predictive_test.go` - `validatePredictive`: the mode enum, the horizon range and the HPA rejection.
6. `pkg/controller/podautoscaler/types/metrics.go` and `metrics/client.go` (+25 together) - the two accessors (`Snapshot`, `GetMetricSeries`) that hand the recorded series to the fit. The window statistics keep feeding the reactive algorithms unchanged.

Everything else is mechanical or documentation, safe to skim: `zz_generated.deepcopy.go`, both `autoscaling.aibrix.ai_podautoscalers.yaml` copies and the five `pkg/client/applyconfiguration` files are produced by `make manifests` and `hack/update-codegen.sh`; `docs/source/features/autoscaling/predictive-autoscaling.rst` and `samples/autoscaling/predictive-kpa.yaml` are the fastest way to see the intended user experience. `autoscaler_predictive_test.go` and `predictive_status_test.go` cover the composition and the status in the same order.

What is worth checking closely: (a) without `spec.predictive` nothing is projected and `status.predictive` stays absent, and in `Preview` the replica decision stays with the reactive path, so the "no behaviour change" claim holds; (b) the floor can only raise `DesiredReplicas`, never lower it, and the scale-up rate caps it the same way it caps the reactive recommendations; (c) a projection is only reported once three samples inside the window span at least half of it, and it is clamped at zero and at four times the observed mean; (d) the HPA strategy is rejected instead of silently ignored.

# Code review

Review of #2804 (draft, head `63fdd26`). Full write-up is in the review notes; this comment is the correctness pass.

`Preview` is a compatible opt-in. With the block omitted, `applyPredictiveFloor` returns the reactive result unchanged, and the validating webhook rejects the block for `scalingStrategy: HPA`. `Auto` is not ready to merge as specified. The forecast is anchored at the mean of the window, so `horizonSeconds` is not a lead time from now, and the floor is applied after dampers that exist to hold a scale-up down.

### The horizon is measured from the window centroid

`leastSquares` returns the slope and the mean of the samples. `Predict` then does:

```go
predicted := observed + slope*horizon.Seconds()
```

(`pkg/controller/podautoscaler/prediction/predictor.go:143`)

That is the value of the fitted line at centroid + horizon, not at now + horizon. For samples spread across the window, the centroid sits about half a window before now, so the lead time past now is `horizon - window/2`.

The new unit test locks this in. Samples at t−30s, t−20s, t−10s, and t with values 10, 20, 30, 40 have slope 1/s and mean 25. The test expects 55 at a 30s horizon (`TestLinearPredictProjectsObservedTrend`). The line at now + 30s is 70. 55 is the line 15s ahead of now, because the centroid is 15s in the past.

The sample manifest uses the same ratio as the defaults: `observeWindowSeconds: 180` and `horizonSeconds: 120` (`samples/autoscaling/predictive-kpa.yaml`). On a full window the centroid is ~90s before now, so the configured two-minute horizon lands ~30s after now. #1418 is about covering model startup, which is longer than that. A horizon shorter than half the window evaluates a time that is still inside the observed window.

The growth cap is `4 * mean` (`MaxGrowthRatio`, applied at `predictor.go:147`). It is coherent only while "observed" is treated as the current level. Once the forecast is evaluated at now + horizon, a steady ramp can put the latest sample above `4 * mean`, and the cap then pulls the forecast back under the current level so the floor never trips. Re-base the cap on the value at now when the anchor is fixed.

Fix the evaluation, then update the test to expect the value at now + horizon:

```text
predicted = mean + slope * ((now - centroid) + horizon)
```

### The floor undoes the pending-replica guard and uses a different scale-up cap

`ComputeDesiredReplicas` runs the guard, then the floor (`autoscaler.go:233-234`). The guard lowers a scale-up while replicas are pending and not yet in the metric. The floor then takes `max(that, projected)` and can put the recommendation back above the guarded value. `PendingReplicaGuardActive` stays true on the copied result, so status can say the guard is active while the desired count ignores it.

The rate cap inside `applyPredictiveFloor` (`autoscaler.go:364-373`) is a third copy of the strategy bound, and it does not match either algorithm:

- KPA caps with `max(1, ceil(maxScaleUpRate * readyPodsCount))` (`algorithm/kpa.go:117`). The floor uses `CurrentReplicas`, which counts pods that are not ready. While pods are pending, the floor's cap is higher than KPA's, which is the same window in which the guard is trying to slow down.
- APA caps with `ceil(maxScaleUpRate * currentPodCount)` and leaves a zero-replica workload at 0 (`algorithm/apa.go:92`). The floor forces `maxUp` to at least 1 whenever the rate is positive.
- A non-positive rate skips the new cap entirely (`if rate > 0`). KPA still clamps through `math.Max(1, ceil(rate * ready))`.

Apply the projected replica count inside the per-metric recommendation, then let the existing guard, `stabilizeRecommendation`, and min/max bounds run once. Cooldown and `minReplicas` / `maxReplicas` / schedules do run later in `computeScaleDecision` (`podautoscaler_controller.go:1216-1234`). That part of the PR description holds, and nothing in this diff tests it.

### Scale-from-zero does not reach the new path

`TestPredictiveAutoWakesWorkloadFromZeroReplicas` calls `applyPredictiveFloor` directly and expects a desired count of 1. The reconciler returns before any metric computation when `currentReplicas == 0` and `minReplicas != 0` unless an active schedule sets min (`podautoscaler_controller.go:1163-1178`). The sample sets `minReplicas: 1`, so that test's situation never enters `ComputeDesiredReplicas`, and `status.predictive` is cleared on every reconcile because the early `ScaleDecision` carries no projection.

APA cannot wake from zero even inside the helper. `predictedReplicas` uses `ceil(currentReplicas * value / targetValue)`, which is 0 when `currentReplicas` is 0, and `evaluatePrediction` then drops the source (`autoscaler.go:441-442`). The KPA-only `maxUp = 1` branch is the only wake path, and it is unreachable for the usual min-replicas setting.

### Status

`status.predictive` is optional and is removed when the spec block is removed. String metric values at 3 decimal places match `targetValue` being a string and keep `lastUpdated` stable across resyncs (`keepPredictiveObservationTime`). That part is sound.

Gaps:

- `computeScaleDecision` replaces the reason with `"<algorithm> above target"` whenever the desired count changes (`podautoscaler_controller.go:1237-1240`). The `"predictive floor=N for <metric>"` suffix written at `autoscaler.go:379` never reaches the event or the status. `PredictiveStatus` has no metric name and no applied bit, so `Auto` is not distinguishable from a reactive scale-up in the API.
- A projection with `predictedReplicas <= 0` is discarded, so `Preview` stays empty when the line forecasts zero. `predictedReplicas` is `omitempty`, so an explicit 0 would disappear from the object too.
- `lastUpdated` is `time.Now()` inside `keepPredictiveObservationTime`, while the fit uses `request.Timestamp` (`r.nowTime()`). A test clock injected on the reconciler does not own the status timestamp.
- Boundary early-returns and a failed metric computation pass a nil projection into `setStatus`, which clears a previously good `status.predictive`.

### API and compatibility

The API delta is additive on `v1alpha1`: optional `spec.predictive` and `status.predictive`, no renamed fields, pointer `horizonSeconds` so unset stays distinct from a value, empty `mode` defaulted to `Preview` in the controller. The CRD enum is `Preview` | `Auto`, and the webhook repeats that check plus the horizon range `1..3600`, consistent with the other window fields. HPA is rejected in the webhook and skipped again at runtime, which is the right behavior for a strategy that writes a native `HorizontalPodAutoscaler`. Generated deepcopy, CRDs, and applyconfiguration match the types. Workloads that omit the block keep today's decision.

### Tests

The new tests are real and cover the behavior the code implements: preview vs auto, the floor not lowering a decision, the local rate cap, sparse history, the KPA/APA ceil formulas, status stability, and the webhook rejects. `TestComputeDesiredReplicasReportsPredictiveAcrossPipeline` walks the autoscaler pipeline with one ready pod, so the pending guard does not run.

Missing, and each one is a bug above:

- Forecast at now + horizon, with `horizon < window/2` and with the sample's 180/120 pair.
- Floor composed with the pending-replica guard, and the cap compared with KPA's ready-pod cap.
- `computeScaleDecision` at 0 replicas with `minReplicas: 1`, including that `status.predictive` survives or is intentionally omitted.
- APA at `currentReplicas == 0`.
- A scale-up event whose reason still says the floor was applied, after `stabilizeRecommendation` and max replicas.

The PR body describes integration and e2e commits. They are not in this diff. Unit coverage is the right bar while the API is settling in #1418; the anchor and the guard ordering should be fixed before `Auto` is something the sample recommends. The sample already uses `mode: Preview`, which is the right default to document.

# Design simplification

The user-facing contract is small and should stay small. The implementation around it is a second scaling pipeline.

### Simpler structure

One function, called from the loop that already exists.

1. Keep `Project(series, now, window, horizon) (value, ok)` in `pkg/controller/podautoscaler/prediction`. It filters to the window, requires 3 points spanning half the window, fits the line, evaluates it at now + horizon, and clamps at 0 and at the growth cap.
2. Inside the existing per-metric loop in `ComputeDesiredReplicas`, convert that value with the strategy formula and take `max(reactiveReplicas, projectedReplicas)` for that metric. The loop already selects the metric that asks for the most replicas.
3. Leave `applyPendingReplicaGuard`, `stabilizeRecommendation`, and the min/max/schedule clamps where they are, operating on that single number.

`Preview` is the same projection written to status with the max step skipped. `Auto` is the max step. No separate floor function, no second walk over `metricsSources`, no second rate-limit formula.

### What to cut

- The `Predictor` interface and the `Linear` fields `MinDataPoints`, `MinCoverage`, and `MaxGrowthRatio`. There is one implementation, and nothing sets those fields except `NewLinear`. Package constants are the whole configuration surface; `horizonSeconds` is already the user knob.
- `evaluatePrediction` as its own pass. It rebuilds `MetricKey` and target lookup that `computeReplicasForSingleMetric` just did.
- `PredictiveResult` fields that never reach the API: `Applied`, `Reason`, `Horizon`, and the internal `MetricName` used only to build a reason string that `computeScaleDecision` throws away. Put `metric` on `status.predictive` if operators need to know which source won. Log the slope at V(4) from the `Project` result.
- The scale-up cap and the `maxUp < 1` special case inside `applyPredictiveFloor`. They duplicate `kpa.go` and `apa.go` and disagree with both.
- The `predictedReplicas <= 0` continue. Report the projection. The floor comparison is what decides whether the desired count moves.
- A plugin point for future models. #1418 asks for a time-series forecast used as an early scale-up. A linear fit with the guards above is that forecast. Another model can replace `Project` later without an interface today.

### What to keep

- `spec.predictive.mode` and `spec.predictive.horizonSeconds`, default mode `Preview`, and `status.predictive` cleared when the spec block is removed.
- Up-only composition: the projection may raise the desired count and the reactive path keeps scale-down, cooldown, and the replica bounds.
- The webhook rejection of HPA, plus the one-line runtime skip for clusters with the webhook disabled.
- The three forecast guards: at least three samples, coverage of at least half the window, clamp at zero and at a growth cap. They are the right safety rail for a line fit on a 10s resync.
- Stable `lastUpdated` while the formatted projection is unchanged, and string metric values, consistent with `targetValue`.
- `MetricHistory.Snapshot` and `GetMetricSeries`. The reactive path only exposes window aggregates; the fit needs the points. The copy under the history lock is the right sharing.
- The docs page, the autoscaling overview row, and `samples/autoscaling/predictive-kpa.yaml` left on `Preview`.
- Generated deepcopy, CRDs, and applyconfiguration. They match the types.

Do not add confidence blending, a panic-window forecast, or extra modes. Schedules already cover a daily shape, and the PR is right to leave them as bounds rather than feeding them into the line.
