# Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct-DSpark

A **DSpark draft model** for speculative decoding with [`unsloth/Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct`](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct) as the verifier, trained with [`speculators`](https://github.com/vllm-project/speculators). The drafter proposes 8 tokens at a time and the verifier checks them in one forward pass, so output is identical to running the verifier alone — a lossless speedup. Mean acceptance length is **3.41** tokens committed per verification step, up to **5.53** on math_reasoning.

Training code: [rasyosef/train-dspark-draft-models](https://github.com/rasyosef/train-dspark-draft-models).

> Trained on 100,000 samples.

## Usage

vLLM loads the verifier automatically from the config — don't pass it separately.

```bash
vllm serve rasyosef/Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct-DSpark --port 8000 --gpu-memory-utilization 0.8
```

Then query the OpenAI-compatible endpoint at `http://localhost:8000/v1`.

## Details

5 Qwen3 layers (hidden size 3072, intermediate size 8192, 24 attention heads over 8 KV heads, sliding-window attention with a 2048-token window), ~0.9B params, bfloat16. Block size 8, draft vocabulary reduced to 50,000, aux hidden-state layers 2/8/14/20/26, confidence head with Markov (rank 256).

Trained for 3 epochs at lr 3e-4 (AdamW, cosine schedule with 4% warmup) on 100,000 [Open PerfectBlend](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mlabonne/open-perfectblend) prompts regenerated by the verifier itself, split 96/4 into train and validation, with a `{"ce": 0.1, "tv": 0.9}` loss. Prompts prepared at 1024 tokens; training sequence length 8192, up to 1024 anchors per sample. Verifier hidden states were pulled on demand from a running vLLM server during training and deleted after use rather than staged to disk up front. speculators `0.8.0.dev207`, vLLM `0.28.0`, transformers `5.15.1`, torch `2.13.0`.

## Evaluation

**Weighted across all subsets: 3.412** over 69,061 verification steps.

Acceptance is still highest where the verifier's next token is most predictable — math, code, structured tool calls. math_reasoning leads HumanEval by about half a token and holds a margin at every position in the block; math_reasoning's pos_5 (43.3%) is above summarization's pos_1 (40.5%), and both subsets still commit better than one token in four at pos_7. tool_call sits on its own at 3.49, between the two groups.

Five of the six prose-like subsets start in a tight band at pos_0 (65.6–67.7%) and separate only deeper into the block; qa is the exception, starting well below the rest at 55.0% and staying last throughout. question and writing decay gently and are still above 4% at pos_7. rag has the strongest pos_1 of the group (45.8%) but falls away after pos_3, and summarization, translation, and qa are all under 1.5% at pos_7 — so the tail of the block is worth something on open-ended generation but essentially nothing on summarization, translation, and qa.

## Limitations

Works only with `Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct` and is not usable as a standalone model. Acceptance falls off steeply past the first few positions on prose-like traffic (summarization, translation, qa), so a block size of 8 is mostly wasted there — the gains concentrate in math, code, and tool calls. Real-world speedup depends on your traffic mix, and because verification is lossless, the verifier's own behavior and biases carry through unchanged.

# gemma-4-E2B-it-DSpark

Model Link: https://huggingface.co/rasyosef/gemma-4-E2B-it-dspark

A **DSpark draft model** for speculative decoding with [`google/gemma-4-E2B-it`](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-E2B-it) as the verifier, trained with [`speculators`](https://github.com/vllm-project/speculators). The drafter proposes 8 tokens at a time and the verifier checks them in one forward pass, so output is identical to running the verifier alone. This makes it a lossless speedup. Mean acceptance length is **2.89** tokens committed per verification step, up to **5.53** on math_reasoning.

Training code: [rasyosef/train-dspark-draft-models](https://github.com/rasyosef/train-dspark-draft-models).

> Trained on 100,000 samples.

## Usage

vLLM loads the verifier automatically from the config, so don't pass it separately.

```bash
vllm serve rasyosef/gemma-4-E2B-it-dspark --port 8000 --gpu-memory-utilization 0.8
```

Then query the OpenAI-compatible endpoint at `http://localhost:8000/v1`.

## Details

**Architecture.** A Qwen3-style decoder with 5 draft layers, ~0.25B params, bfloat16. It has hidden size 1536, MLP size 6144, and 12 query heads over 2 KV heads (GQA, head dim 128). The first four layers use sliding-window attention (window 2048) and the last uses full attention. Block size is 8. The draft vocabulary is reduced to 50,000 tokens from the verifier's full 262,144, selected by token frequency over the training data. Aux hidden states come from verifier layers 2/11/22/33.

**Training.** 3 epochs at lr 3e-4 (AdamW, cosine schedule with 4% warmup) with a `{"ce": 0.1, "tv": 0.9}` loss. The data is 100,000 filtered [Open PerfectBlend](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mlabonne/open-perfectblend) prompts regenerated by the verifier itself, split 96/4 into train and validation. Prompts were prepared at 768 tokens. Training sequence length was 8192, with up to 1024 anchors per sample. Verifier hidden states were pulled on demand from a running vLLM server during training and deleted after use, rather than staged to disk up front.

Acceptance is highest where the verifier's next token is most predictable, and math_reasoning is in a class of its own. It leads HumanEval by nearly 1.7 tokens and still accepts more than half of pos_4 drafts (51.0%). Its last position (pos_7, 31.4%) sits above HumanEval's pos_4 (25.6%). math_reasoning is the one subset that makes real use of the full 8-token block. HumanEval is clearly second at 3.84, well ahead of everything else.

tool_call leads the remaining subsets at 2.62, with the highest pos_0 outside math and code (61.3%). It decays faster than prose, though, and falls behind writing and question from pos_4 onward. writing, question, and translation form a tight band (2.43–2.57). rag opens strongly at 58.1%, above question and translation. It then decays fastest of the mid-pack, dropping below 1% by pos_7, and lands at 2.31 just above qa (2.19). summarization is the clear outlier: it starts lowest at 47.2%, drops to 20.0% by pos_1, and is under 1% from pos_5 on.

Outside math and code, positions 5–7 each add at most about 7 percentage points. Nearly all of the gain on general traffic comes from the first three or four positions.

## Limitations

Works only with `gemma-4-E2B-it` and is not usable as a standalone model. Acceptance falls off steeply past the first two positions on prose-like traffic, most of all on summarization, so the gains concentrate in math and code. On prose-heavy workloads the tail of the 8-token block rarely gets accepted. Real-world speedup depends on your traffic mix. Because verification is lossless, the verifier's own behavior and biases carry through unchanged.
