I’m assuming “AI” here doesn’t just mean GPT, Claude, Gemini, and so on. I’m taking it more broadly, as the general idea of using computers to emulate neural networks.

In that sense, I think it’s probably one of the more significant technologies in human history.

But it’s still technology. It’s part of civilization.

Fire, knives, social systems, computer systems, traditions, little bits of everyday wisdom… basically anything can be misused. It has happened before, and I’m pretty sure it will keep happening.

Technology follows the laws of physics. It doesn’t automatically represent good or evil, the goals of a group, or the common good. Physics doesn’t do that for us either. This is a pretty old theme, from the classics all the way to [Reconguista in G](https://en.gundam-official.com/series/reconguista-in-g).

For that matter, I find good and evil useful concepts, but in my own worldview they are basically convenient abstractions, and fairly arbitrary ones.

Some biological mechanisms that help groups function, such as conscience, recognizing other minds, and empathy, seem to go back hundreds of millions of years, roughly to the evolutionary level of fish.

And after hundreds of millions of years… well, here we are.

Still very far from perfect. Even with the good and evil patch installed, the current build is pretty janky.

So I don’t have especially high expectations for what humanity as a whole will accomplish with AI.

On a smaller scale, though, I think it can be great.

If someone is reasonably good at choosing goals that make sense for their actual life, and they don’t dislike other beings, including cats, they might use fire and knives to cook good food and make good use of resources.

I think AI is probably similar.

But I don’t expect the whole species to use it that way.

There is also the option of imposing stronger rules on everyone and trying to force the group toward some collective objective. Personally, I dislike that even more than people misusing technology.

Mostly because humanity has done it so many times that I find it boring and cliché. And quite often the collective objective itself is something I find boring too, or a topic I could happily spend my whole life not caring about.

So, coming back to the original question, what do I think about AI?

For me, it’s a fun toy that keeps changing.

It’s a fast changing new technology, and new technology has always been a kind of toy for me. I’m not an engineer or a researcher, and I’m not particularly skilled at using technology.

I just enjoy using it.

The speed of AI development may create some risks that are unusual compared with other technologies. It probably does.

But risk management isn’t something I’m personally interested in. The people who are paid to deal with that can deal with it however they think they should. They’re going to make those decisions themselves anyway.

So I’ll just keep playing with AI out of curiosity.

I’m not interested in attacking people, so I don’t use it for attacks.

AI is an incredible tool, but it’s important to remember what’s actually happening under the hood:

-
**Frozen Weights:**Once an LLM finishes training, its weights are strictly read-only. The model doesn’t learn, adapt, or rewrite its own code while chatting with you. -
**No Active Inner Life:**There’s no mind running in the background when you close the tab. It only “thinks” during the exact split second it takes to run a forward mathematical pass and predict the next token in response to your prompt. -
**Context Limits:**Because memory is bound by a strict context window and parameters are static, current models can’t naturally evolve or build true continuous understanding.

They are powerful pattern-matching tools, not self-updating intelligences!

That’s a classic sci-fi scenario, but from an edge AI perspective, we’re nowhere near that reality. The real roadblock isn’t the hardware or cameras—it’s **local context memory**.

A robot patrolling a dynamic street generates gigabytes of visual and spatial tokens every minute. It can’t rely on the cloud for real-time safety, so it must process everything locally. But mobile, battery-powered chips can’t handle a massive, growing context loop without running out of RAM or draining the battery in minutes.

To make this work, we’d need to compress multi-gigabyte context streams down to fit on embedded memory that is **100x smaller**, all without the robot losing situational awareness or glitching. Until we solve that massive memory bandwidth bottleneck, fully autonomous robot police patrolling open streets are easily **10+ years away**.

That memory bottleneck point makes the idea of fully autonomous robot police feel much more complicated than it first sounds.
