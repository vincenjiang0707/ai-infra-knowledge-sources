## Description

Promised in the review of #3907: a design document for the deadline machinery, which until now had no entry of its own. Device selection is documented in `slice-spraying.md`; the admission queue and the RDMA bandwidth arbitration were mentioned only in passing inside its transmit-estimate section, and of the eleven `runtime_queue/*` keys one appeared anywhere in the docs.

`docs/source/design/tent/deadline-scheduling.md` covers, in order: the path a `deadline_ns` takes from `Request` to metric; the shared `DeadlineMlu` predictor and why the two layers read different `bytes_ahead`; the admission queue (capacity limits, EDF insert, slack promotion, deadline-infeasible drop, eligibility, dispatch window and the progress-worker fallback timer, the bandwidth provider); the RDMA arbitration (`orderByDeadline`, posted-bytes queue-ahead, slot-by-slot greedy order within a priority tier); the two bandwidth series and why per-completion timing cannot feed the predictors; a three-way comparison with device selection; every configuration key with type, default and meaning, plus the request fields and C++/Python examples; the two metrics; where each effect is observable (drop and arbitration act on RDMA only); and the known limits (no drop hook installed, no lower bound on the threshold, mixed-submit bypass, quarantined-owner bytes, no RDMA completion wake).

Two small edits alongside: `overview.md` gets a "TENT Deadline Scheduling" toctree entry, and `slice-spraying.md`'s transmit-estimate section links to the new page; one phrase there claimed the arbitration's 64 exactly-resolved slots are "more than one post can take", which is not so with the default `max_qp_wr` of 256, and is corrected. The `deadline_ns` comment in `types.h` still said the field "does not yet drive any admission or scheduling decision"; it now names what reads it and points at the document.
