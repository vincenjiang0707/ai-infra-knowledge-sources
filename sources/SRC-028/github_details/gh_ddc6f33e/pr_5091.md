# [PR #5091] Speed up `shard_mode: explicit`: kernel-order weight gradients and a vocab-parallel LM head

## 正文

## What

Three flags that make `shard_mode: explicit` faster, plus the ~2,900-line study that produced them
(`docs/guides/optimization/shard_mode_performance.md`).

| flag | default | acts under |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `lm_head_weight_grad_in_kernel_order` | on | `explicit` |
| `dense_weight_grad_in_kernel_order` | on | `explicit` |
| `lm_head_vocab_parallel` | on for untied heads | **both** modes |
| `save_fsdp_gathered_weights` | off | **both** modes |

With them, at a production sequence length **`explicit` is faster than `auto` on the best
configuration available to every one of the eight onboarded models**, on shipped defaults in both
arms, with losses identical or within ±0.001.

## Why

JAX's `lower_with_sharding_in_types` annotates every sharding-in-types primitive's output with a
`custom_call_target="Sharding"` op — 65 of them under `auto` on llama2 versus 1,276 under
`explicit`. One lands between a weight-gradient `dot` and the `transpose` that follows it, and XLA's
algebraic simplifier will not fold `transpose(dot(A,B)) → dot(B,A)` across it. Census on the dp4
module: **auto has 17 adjacent (foldable) transpose-of-dot pairs, explicit has 0.**

The surviving transpose costs twice: it leaves the gradient sharded on its minor-most dimension,
below the ~512-byte per-shard extent `tpu-all-reduce-scatter-fusion` requires (so the weight-gradient
all-reduce is never rewritten into a reduce-scatter — 2× the wire bytes), and its layout propagates
through the whole Adam update (196.6 MB/step of relayout copies on llama2, 1.57 GB/step at emb 4096).

The barrier itself cannot be removed: it is JAX's, not MaxText's. Gating MaxText's `out_sharding`
pin produces a 0-line HLO diff.

## How

`DenseGeneral` gains `weight_grad_in_kernel_order`, backed by a `custom_vjp` whose backward pass
contracts `dk` straight into the kernel's stored axis order, so there is no transpose to fold.
The stored kernel, its sharding and its initialization are untouched — **checkpoint-compatible**;
the gradient differs from the default rule only by floating-point reassociation. It self-disables
under `auto` (where XLA folds the transpose itself) and under quantization.

`lm_head_vocab_parallel` shards the untied head's output on `vocab` instead of gathering logits;
`save_fsdp_gathered_weights` keeps the FSDP-gathered kernel as a `config.dtype` remat residual so the
backward pass does not re-gather it. Both are mode-neutral and measured under both modes.

## Measured

TPU7x (Ironwood), 8 chips, d16 (`base_emb_dim=2048 base_mlp_dim=8192`, 8Q/8KV heads, `head_dim=128`),
L12, `ici_fsdp_parallelism=8`, synthetic data, checkpointing off, **nothing written out but
`shard_mode`** — both arms on shipped defaults. Median of the profiled `jit_train_step` module spans,
3 reps, rep spread 0.00–0.05%.

| model | `auto` ns | `explicit` ns | seq 4096 | seq 1024 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| qwen3-8b | 76,733,270 | 75,853,184 | **−1.147%** | +0.366% |
| llama2-7b | 67,042,013 | 66,662,397 | −0.566% | −0.460% |
| gemma-2b | 89,692,621 | 89,240,383 | −0.504% | −0.491% |
| gemma2-2b | 159,056,838 | 158,264,830 | −0.498% | +0.524% |
| gemma3-4b | 92,265,701 | 91,923,323 | −0.371% | +0.266% |
| mistral-7b | 66,884,694 | 66,675,081 | −0.313% | −0.250% |
| mixtral-8x7b | 225,684,289 | 225,471,856 | −0.094% | −0.064% |
| deepseek2-16b | 1,367,040,531 | 1,367,731,966 | +0.051% | −0.061% |
| | | **median** | **−0.435%** | −0.063% |

**Read the two columns differently.** At seq 1024 this is a wash (median −0.063%, three models
regress). At seq 4096 every model that regressed at 1024 wins, and the two biggest swings are the two
biggest 1024-token losses. The whole 1024-token deficit is *one collective* — the MLP kernels' FSDP
all-gather, identical count / shape / layout / bytes in both modes, 2.3% longer async window under
explicit. It is a fixed cost, so more tokens per step dilutes it. Three independent checks agree:
`save_fsdp_gathered_weights` in **both** arms collapses the three 1024-token regressions to ±0.04%;
`fsdp4 × tp2` flips them negative; and Table B above.

**deepseek2-16b** is the one row still positive at seq 4096 / fsdp 8 (+0.051%, ~1.7× rep spread).
No flag in this PR causes it — turning each off individually leaves it where it was. On
`ici_fsdp_parallelism=4 ici_expert_parallelism=2`, the mesh a 64-expert MoE would actually run on and
6.1% faster than fsdp 8 in *both* modes, it is **−0.059%**.

**Control for the libtpu flag set.** The above is stock libtpu. Repeating the whole matrix under the
31-flag production SparseCore set — which offloads exactly the collective the deficit lives in —
still gives explicit 7 of 8, median −0.118%, with the arms swapped: deepseek2 becomes a win at fsdp 8
and qwen3 becomes the lone +0.046%. **No model loses under both flag sets.** That set is itself a
1–5% regression at seq 4096, so it is not the configuration to run there.

**The flags are load-bearing.** gemma2-2b under explicit at seq 1024: shipped defaults +0.524%,
`dense_weight_grad_in_kernel_order=false` +1.126%, both kernel-order flags off +1.145%.

### Numerical equivalence

12 steps, synthetic data, `auto` vs `explicit` loss at every step. At seq 4096 mistral-7b, gemma3-4b
and mixtral-8x7b print **identical** trajectories and the other five agree to the last printed digit
(±0.001) at every step with no growth in the difference. Under the SparseCore arms the same check
passes on a different partition of the models (four identical, four within ±0.001), which is what
reassociation looks like and not what a correctness bug looks like. Earlier 20-step runs against the
`auto` baseline put max absolute loss deviation at 2.52e-04 (llama2) and 9.63e-05 (mixtral) — at or
below explicit's own noise level.

## Negative results, documented rather than shipped

Recorded in §8 so the next person does not spend the time. Each was implemented or measured, not
reasoned about: `lm_head_kernel_transposed` (works, but slower on 4 of 7 configs, no
checkpoint-conversion path, and it changes initialization); eliding no-op `reshard` barriers
(op-identical executable — XLA already discards them); widening the `auto_axes` region from one dot
to the whole `MlpBlock` (+0.366% → +0.359%, inside rep spread); `unreduced={'data'}` GA hoisting
(traces and fires, and buys nothing — XLA already sinks the all-reduce); `jax.lax.psum_scatter` and a
`custom_vjp` wrapping it (slower, and the latter is *wrong* — 4× the gradient); ZeRO-1 as an
explicit-only win (unreachable — mutually exclusive with FSDP in both modes); and tensor parallelism
as explicit's standing win (does not survive a fair comparison at healthy depth).

The study also turned up two mode-neutral findings larger than anything mode-specific in it:
`param_scan_axis: 1` degenerates when the layer-stack length is a multiple of 8, worth ~25% of step
time in **both** modes (§4.9), and the YaRN frequency table was being rebuilt inside every layer
(§5.5, fixed).

## Notes

- The `explicit`-mode defaults changed since the first revision of this PR: both kernel-order flags
  now default on under `explicit`, and `lm_head_vocab_parallel` defaults on for untied heads under
  **both** modes. `save_fsdp_gathered_weights` stays off — it costs HBM, and §4.11 records where it
  pays and where it does not.
- §4.11's "leave `save_fsdp_gathered_weights` off on gemma2/gemma3" rule is a v5p-8 result that does
  **not** reproduce on TPU7x, where the flag is −10% to −19% on those same models across both libtpu
  sets, both remat policies and both FSDP degrees. Flagged in the doc as an open discrepancy rather
  than reconciled, since the hardware is the one variable that could not be controlled.
