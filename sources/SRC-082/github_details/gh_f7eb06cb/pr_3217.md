## 正文

## Summary

Accelerate GGUF Q6_K BF16 prefill for small-output projections by reusing the existing packed Metal decoder across a four-row tile. On the Qwen3.8-27B K/V projections at 16 rows, the complete layer path is 1.152–1.174× faster than current `main`.

The dispatch is deliberately narrow: BF16, 2–16 rows, and at most 2048 output features. FP16, larger outputs, and longer prefills retain the current two-affine-matmul path because the packed kernel did not win consistently there.

## What Changed

- Generalize the existing packed Q6_K kernel call to multiple row tiles.
- Select one 32-lane SIMD group for the proven small-output BF16 prefill range.
- Preserve current direct-dtype output, bias behavior, decode dispatch, and fallback behavior.
- Add neighboring-FP-value, signed-zero, code-endpoint, bias, FP16, and BF16 oracle coverage for decode and small prefill.
- Add FP16/BF16 prefill regression coverage for every Qwen3.8-27B projection.
- Add a reproducible current-main benchmark with separate FP32/raw, visible/rounded, and visible/raw drift measurements.
- No public API changes. Other GGUF formats are out of scope.

## Accuracy and Performance

Environment: Mac Studio M5 Ultra, 256 GB, MLX 0.32.2. Baseline: `1f3d8bf`. Inputs and packed weights were resident. Timings are medians of 301 interleaved samples after 10 warmups and include the complete linear-layer call.

`native` identifies the two cases whose dispatch changes. `unchanged` cases retain the current-main implementation; their timing ratios show measurement noise rather than a claimed optimization. The oracle independently decodes Q6_K values and performs the dot product in Torch FP64.

Their FP32 arithmetic differs from the independent FP64 oracle by at most `7.9334773e-08`; maximum visible/raw drift is `2.7702398e-05`, over 72× below the `2e-3` inference limit. Across every projection and both dtypes, maximum visible/rounded drift is `6.1035156e-05` and maximum visible/raw drift is `3.8024319e-05`.
